# Arabia RPG

A small browser RPG for exploring a pre-Islamic Arabian oasis town at sunset: a walled mudbrick town with tower houses and a columned shrine, a market of striped stalls outside the gate, palms around a pool, a Bedouin camp, a Nabataean rock-cut tomb, and dune country with caravans. The art style is low-poly and voxel-like (box people, box camels and horses). See `docs/references/mood_still.png` for the look.

## Game scope (first playable)
- **Explore:** walk around the town and market. A high third-person camera follows the player and looks down on them.
- **Talk to strangers:** walk up to an NPC and press a key to open dialogue. NPCs have a role (merchant, guard, priest, wanderer, water carrier and so on) with flavour lines that suit the setting.
- **Sword:** the player carries a sword. Attacking plays a swing animation (`Attack` / `Attack2`). A hit damages NPCs, who play `Hit`, and at 0 HP they play `Death` and stay down. Hostility and reactions can stay simple at first.
- **Horse:** walk up to a horse and press a key to mount. The player rides it and can gallop. Dismount with the same key. Attacking from horseback should work, because the upper-body clips layer over `Ride`.

## Engine & stack (decided)
**three.js + TypeScript + Vite**, with **Rapier** (`@dimforge/rapier3d-compat`) for collision and the character controller.

Why this stack:
- It produces the smallest web build.
- It loads glTF natively, so the GLB assets drop straight in.
- It gives full control over an orthographic or perspective camera.
- It has the largest ecosystem and examples.
- It works well with a code-only, agent-driven workflow.

Babylon.js was the runner-up (more batteries included). Godot's web export was rejected: the download is large and it has browser and threading quirks. Pin exact package versions.

## Camera & controls
- **Camera:** an orthographic, high-angle "isometric-ish" camera in the style of Shadowrun Returns (`docs/references/camera_orthographic_shadowrun*.png`). It follows the player smoothly. Zoom is optional, and a perspective toggle is nice to have.
- **Movement:** WASD moves relative to the camera's screen directions. With a rotated camera, W must mean screen-up, not world +Z.
- **Facing (hard requirement):** the character must face exactly the direction it is moving. `docs/references/movement_facing_bug.png` shows the bug to avoid: holding S made the character run diagonally or face the wrong way. Derive the yaw from the camera-relative input vector, `atan2(dir.x, dir.z)` (models face +Z). Rotate toward it quickly with shortest-angle interpolation, and never let an animation or physics step drift it.
- Match foot speed to movement speed using the clip speeds listed below.

## Assets (`assets/`, generated: do not hand-edit)
Everything is generated by headless Blender from `tools/blender/`. `assets/manifest.json` lists every file with its size, materials, clips and notes.

**Conventions:** metres; +Y is up; models face +Z with their origin at the bottom centre. A Blender point (x, y, z) becomes three.js (x, z, -y). Yaw values in the JSON are rotations about +Y.

**`characters/person.glb`**
- One skeleton: root, hips, spine, head, arm_L/R, hand_L/R, leg_L/R.
- A `Body` SkinnedMesh plus 19 accessory SkinnedMeshes, toggled with `.visible`. Each NPC needs its own `SkeletonUtils.clone`.
  - Head: Beard, BeardLong, Hair, Keffiyeh, Turban, Veil, Headband, FaceWrap, Helmet, Jar.
  - Body: Vest, Lamellar, Cloak, Belly (a heavier build), Waterskin, Bow (carried on the back).
  - Weapons: Sword and Spear in hand_R, and Shield on the left forearm (hand_L).
  - For an older man, recolour `P_Hair` grey; it covers both beards and the hair.
- Recolour by cloning the materials named `P_*`: P_Robe, P_Sash, P_Pants, P_Skin, P_Wrap, P_Hair, P_Mask, P_Band, P_Vest, P_Armor, P_ArmorTrim, P_Cloak, P_Shield, P_ShieldPaint and so on.
- Clips: Idle, Walk (1.5 m/s), Run (4.8 m/s), Attack, Attack2, HeavyAttack, Thrust, Block (loop), BlockHit, Hit, Stagger, Knockdown, GetUp, Backstep, Roll, Jump, Death (non-looping; use `clampWhenFinished`), Talk, Ride, Sit.
- `manifest.json` → `assets.person.clips.<name>` gives `duration`, `loop`, `layer` and, for attacks, `hit_time` (HeavyAttack also has `windup_end_time`). Use those values instead of hard-coding them.
- Each clip only contains tracks for the bones it uses. `layer: "upper"` clips (Attack, Attack2, HeavyAttack, Thrust, Block, BlockHit, Hit, Talk) layer over Walk, Run or Ride. Fade out the base clip's arm and spine contribution if the arms fight.
- Thrust rotates hand_R so the upright Spear levels out. Knockdown ends lying down, in the same pose GetUp starts from.

**`characters/horse.glb`**
- Clips: Idle, Walk (1.7 m/s), Gallop (9 m/s), Rear.
- Accessory: Saddle. Recolour H_Coat and H_Mane.
- Bone `mount`: every frame, put the rider's root at `mount.getWorldPosition()` with the horse's yaw, and play `Ride` on the rider. Don't rely on the bone's orientation.

**`characters/camel.glb`**
- Clips: Idle, Walk, Run, Sit (lying down).
- Accessories: Saddle and Cargo. It has a `mount` bone like the horse.

**`level/`** (the oasis; other maps are listed in `level/maps.json`)
- `maps.json`: `{start, maps: [{name, title, level}]}`. The maps are oasis (`level/level.json`), hegra, caravanserai, wadi and raider_fort (`level/<name>/level.json`). Every level.json uses the same schema, and its file paths are relative to `assets/`. Load whichever files a level lists: `terrain`, `town`, `backdrop`, `backdrop_ground`, `dressing`.
- **Travel:**
  - `exits: [{to, pos, radius, label}]` in each level. Walking into `radius` should travel to map `to`.
  - On arrival, use the target map's `spawns` entry with `role: "player"` and `from: <map you came from>`, falling back to the `player` spawn that has no `from`.
- **New spawn roles:** `farmer` (wadi) and `prisoner` (fort). Treat unknown roles as wanderers. Enemy spawns may carry `title` (for example `"chief"`).
- `dressing.glb` (oasis): rocks, shrubs, acacias, dead trees, ruins, bones and exit cairns out in the dunes. Their colliders are in level.json (tags rock, tree, ruin).
- `terrain.glb`: the playable 400×400 m ground. It has vertex colours and no normals, so GLTFLoader flat-shades it automatically.
- `town.glb`: all static buildings, stalls, palms, the tomb, tents and the pool, joined into one mesh with one draw call per material.
- `backdrop.glb` + `backdrop_ground.glb`: distant mountains, buttes and a ground ring. Visual only, no collision.
- `level.json`:
  - `colliders`: yaw-rotated boxes with `pos` (centre), `half` (half extents) and a `tag`.
  - `spawns`: entries with a `role` (player, wanderer, merchant, sitter, water_carrier, guard, priest, camel, horse), `pos` and `yaw`.
  - `lights`: stall lamps and the campfire.
  - `paths`: the caravan crest and the market lane.
  - `landmarks`.
  - `sun`: the direction toward the sun.
  - `fog`.
  - `bounds`: the playable area.
  - `heights`: describes `heights.bin`, a float32 heightfield for ground queries or a Rapier heightfield. The layout is in `level.json`.
- Use the terrain mesh (trimesh) or the heightfield as ground, plus the box colliders. Clamp the player to `bounds`.

**Gameplay markers and platforming** (all 8 maps)
- **Maps:** oasis, hegra, caravanserai, wadi, raider_fort, rooftops, cliffs, ruined_temple.
- **Platforming maps:** rooftops, cliffs and ruined_temple are built to the jump budget: required moves are at most 1.35 m up with a gap of at most 2.7 m, or a drop with a gap of at most 3.7 m. Secrets sit 2.6–3.2 m up and need the double jump.
- **Walkable surfaces:** every one is a level.json box whose `pos.y + half.y` equals the visible top. Tags: roof, crate, beam, pillar, ledge, aqueduct, wall, sanctuary, stair, architrave, altar, cliff, tower.
- **Marker spawns:**
  - `bonfire` (id)
  - `shopkeeper` (id)
  - `quest_giver` (id, e.g. `oasis_priest`)
  - `pickup` (id, used by fetch quests)
  - `chest` (id, optional `secret`)
  - `winged_horse`
  - Marker `pos.y` is the platform height when the marker sits on a roof, ledge or pillar.
- **`characters/horse_winged.glb`:** the horse rig plus wing_L and wing_R, with the same `mount` bone, ridden bareback. Clips: Idle, Walk, Gallop and Rear (wings folded), plus Flap (a 12-frame loop) and Glide (a 24-frame loop).
- **`props/bonfire.glb`:** nodes `Bonfire` and `Flame` (hide `Flame` while unlit).
- **`props/chest.glb`:** nodes `ChestBase` and `ChestLid`. The lid hinges at its origin; `rotation.x = -1.7` opens it.
- **`tools/blender/platform_maps.py`:** the platform kit (pbox, roof_house, beam, crates, square_pillar) with a `gap_ok` budget check.
- **`preview_glb.py`:** round-trips a GLB at a chosen clip and frame.

**Other assets**
- `buildings/`, `market/`, `nature/`, `props/`: a prop kit of individual GLBs for building more areas. `props/sword.glb` has its grip at the origin and its blade along +Y.
- `sky/sunset_env.hdr`: linear, for lighting through RGBELoader and PMREM as `scene.environment`.
- `sky/sunset_bg.jpg`: already tone-mapped, for `scene.background`. Both are equirectangular in three.js orientation.
- Previews of what the assets should look like: `previews/rigs.png` (all clips and gear) and `previews/level_<map>.png` (one per map).

## Regenerating / extending assets
Blender 5.2.2 portable install: `D:\PROGs\blender-5.2.2-windows-x64\blender.exe`. Run every script headless:
```
blender.exe -b --factory-startup -P tools/blender/export_assets.py -- [only=characters,props,level,maps,sky] [maps=hegra,wadi]
blender.exe -b --factory-startup -P tools/blender/preview_rigs.py      # pose contact sheet
blender.exe -b --factory-startup -P tools/blender/preview_level.py -- [map=hegra] [at=<landmark>] [scale=280] [colliders]
blender.exe -b --factory-startup -P tools/blender/render_still.py -- [preview]
```

What each file does:
- `arabia_lib.py`: shared materials, the `MB` box-mesh builder, `height(x, y)` and every prop builder.
- `rigs.py`: armatures, rigid skinning and the animation clips.
- `export_assets.py`: writes everything plus the manifest. It includes the oasis level, its dune dressing and its exits.
- `kit.py`: map building blocks, each of which registers its own colliders on a `Ctx`:
  - dressing: rocks, shrubs, acacias, ruins, wells, braziers and campfires
  - cliffs, canyons and tomb facades, plus the Khazneh
  - the caravanserai
  - fort pieces: palisades, watchtowers, weapon racks, spikes and dummies
- `maps.py`: `MapDef` subclasses (Hegra, Caravanserai, Wadi, RaiderFort). Each has its own `height()`, ground palette and `build()`. `export_map()` writes them all in the shared schema. To add a map, subclass `MapDef` and add it to `MAPS`.
- `arabia_lib.height()` uses `HEIGHT_FN`, which each map swaps in, so every builder lands on that map's ground.

Rig notes:
- Every bone points +Z with roll 0. In a clip, rotation X > 0 swings a hanging limb backward, and pose `location.y` is up.
- Add new clips with `rigs.clip()`.
- Check the result visually with `preview_rigs.py` before using it.

A live-Blender MCP server is configured in `.mcp.json`. It needs Blender open with the blender_mcp add-on. It's optional; headless scripts are the main path.

Windows quirks: long heredocs that contain Python break in Git Bash, so write scripts to files. `uv` needs `UV_CACHE_DIR=D:\uv-cache` and `UV_LINK_MODE=copy`.

## Working rules
- Verify gameplay in the built-in browser preview: set up `.claude/launch.json` for the Vite dev server, check the console for errors, and take screenshots.
- Test muted: open the preview at `http://localhost:5173/?mute`. The `?mute` flag silences sound effects, ambience and music without changing the volume settings, and it survives reloads. Go back to the plain URL when done.
- Keep performance in mind for about 80 NPCs: share geometry and materials, and use simple per-NPC state machines. Raycasts against the heightfield are cheaper than physics for NPCs.
- Never add Claude attribution, such as Co-Authored-By trailers or "Generated with" lines, to commits, PRs or docs.
- Every new feature ships with all of these. They are acceptance criteria, not extras:
  - **keyboard and gamepad** support, with prompts for both;
  - **on-theme art** (pre-Islamic Arabia; ask the Blender art session for assets, and use code placeholders until they land);
  - **sound effects** (`Sfx` in `fx.ts`);
  - **English and Arabic text** through the localization layer, with Arabic in readable classical style and no harakat except in poetry.

## Game code (`src/`)
Run `npm install` and then `npm run dev` (port 5173; `.claude/launch.json` entry `arabia-dev`). `npm run build` typechecks and bundles to `dist/`. Vite serves `assets/` as the public root, so `assets/level/town.glb` is fetched as `/level/town.glb`. `.npmrc` sets `legacy-peer-deps`, because npm 10 trips over Vite 8's optional peers.

| `sky.ts` | Time of day: sky dome shader (sunset photo/HDR around 18:12), keyframed lights, environment map regenerated from the dome, night colour grade. |
| `nav.ts` | 1 m nav grid rasterised from the level boxes; A* path finding and a path follower. |
| `combat.ts` | Shared hit and player-view types. |
| `enemy.ts` | Raider enemies (raider, skirmisher, brute): telegraphed windup, committed strike, recovery, poise, combos, leashing. |
| `fx.ts` | Hitstop, slow motion, voxel particles, hit and tell flashes, telegraph glints, synthesised WebAudio sounds. |
| `menu.ts` | Pause menu (time of day, character look, robe colour, camera, minimap, volume, controls). |
| `minimap.ts` | Corner minimap rotated to the camera. |
| `physics.ts` | Rapier world and `Mover`, a kinematic capsule. |

Decisions worth knowing:
- **Ground.** Ground height comes from the heightfield, and the Rapier controller only resolves horizontal collisions against the level boxes and actor capsules. If the terrain were part of the controller sweep, the slope-slide would push the character sideways (the `movement_facing_bug.png` drift).
- **Sun.** The sun's azimuth and colour come from level.json, but the light is lifted to at least 24° elevation (`SUN_MIN_ELEVATION_DEG` in `world.ts`). At the authored 8°, one dune shadow covers the whole market.
- **Draw calls.** Characters are merged at spawn into one mesh with two draw groups (matte, metal). Accessories are chosen before the merge and can't be toggled afterwards.
- **Controls** follow Elden Ring's layout. The HUD glyphs follow the last device used.
  - Keyboard: WASD move, Shift sprint, LMB or J attack, RMB block (hold), Space roll, F jump (hold for height), R drink a healing waterskin, E interact (talk, trade, rest, take, travel, mount), Tab menu, M map, Esc menu, middle-mouse drag or Z and C to rotate, wheel zoom. In menus: arrows or WASD, Enter, Esc; Tab, Shift+Tab, `[` and `]` switch tabs.
  - Gamepad (standard mapping): left stick move (analog), B tap roll, B hold or L3 sprint (L3 held, or clicked to stay on), RT attack, LT block / aim (hold) (RB and LB also work), X heal, A jump (hold) and confirm, Y interact, Start menu, View map, right stick camera, D-pad zoom and rotate. In menus: D-pad or stick, A choose, B close, LB and RB switch tabs.
- **Progression** (`progress.ts`, saved to localStorage):
  - **Gold** drops from enemies. On death your gold stays where you fell (`lostGold`); die again before picking it up and it's gone.
  - **Bonfires** (`role: "bonfire"` spawns, else one beside the arrival point): rest to heal, refill waterskins and respawn enemies. The last one you rested at is where you wake. Lit bonfires appear in the menu's Travel tab.
  - **Shop:** a `shopkeeper` spawn, or the merchant nearest the arrival point. It sells weapons (`items.ts`: damage, reach, arc, speed, stamina, clips) and upgrades: vitality, endurance, waterskins, shield, double jump.
- **Quests** (`quests.ts`): kill a camp, talk to someone, or fetch an item. NPCs are referenced by map, spawn role and index. Givers show `!` or `?` icons; objectives are marked on the minimap and the full map (including the exit that leads to another region's objective). The HUD shows the tracked quest.
- **Platforming.** The player lands on and walks along the tops of level boxes (`Physics.support`). The step-up is 0.42 m. Boxes within a step of the feet are left out of the horizontal sweep. The jump is variable (about 1.8 m held), with coyote time and an optional double jump. Sprinting and jumping cost no stamina unless enemies are near (`Player.inCombat`).
- **Menu** (`menu.ts`): tabs for Inventory, Map (full map with a legend), Quests, Travel (lit bonfires, plus a quick level change to any region), Settings and Debug.
  - Debug can:
    - switch to the true third-person camera and set look sensitivity and invert-Y;
    - give the bow and arrows;
    - spawn hounds, vultures, slingers, clubmen, a horse or a winged horse;
    - give gold, toggle god mode and unlock everything;
    - kill nearby enemies, show colliders, teleport to the next exit and start a new game.
- **Stamina.** Attacks, rolls, jumps and sprinting cost stamina, and regen pauses briefly after each action. Rolls have i-frames from 0.06 to 0.42 s.
- **Enemies.** Enemy spawns live in level.json as `role: "enemy"` with a `kind` and a `camp` (from `export_assets.py`). Enemy windups use the Attack and Attack2 overlays, slowed to the raise and then held. There's no separate windup clip.
- **Clips added for the game.** The `Roll` and `Jump` clips are in `rigs.py` and play as one-shot base clips.
- **Maps and travel.**
  - `main.ts` (`enterMap`) loads a map through `world.ts` (`loadMap`, cached for revisits) and rebuilds physics, people, animals and the minimap for it. The player keeps HP, stamina and their look across maps.
  - Exits are marked by a light shaft. The player travels with the interact button (E or Y) rather than by walking in, so nobody leaves by accident.
- **See-through cutout.** Town and dressing materials are patched in `world.ts` (`applyCutout`). Fragments nearer the camera than the player, inside a screen circle of about 2.6 m around them and above their feet, are dither-discarded. The circle is fed each frame by `Game.updateCutout`. Shadows are unaffected.
- **Clip layering.** `characters.ts` reads it from the manifest (`layer`, `loop`), so new clips need no code lists. Enemy swings use each clip's `hit_time`, and HeavyAttack plays its own authored raise and hold.
- **Enemy kinds.**
  - raider: shield and block, with a guard break after two blocked hits.
  - skirmisher: Thrust, and a backstep after combos.
  - brute: HeavyAttack and hyper-armour.
  - chief: a spawn with `title: "chief"`.
  - Heavy blows knock the player down (Knockdown, then GetUp). The player can't be hit while down.
- **Audio.**
  - `fx.ts` (`Sfx`) synthesises every effect: combat, footsteps (sand or stone), hooves, wing beats synced to Flap, animals, chests and coins.
  - It also covers vocal grunts and yelps when people are hit, the spear thrust, jump and double jump, knockdown and getting up, shield raise and guard break, mounting, quest jingles, the shop, travel and the YOU DIED drone. Pass `Sfx` only finite values; it ignores NaN rather than throwing.
  - Dialogue types out at about 42 characters per second, with pauses at punctuation. `Hud.onVoice` voices every other letter as an "animalese" blip (`Sfx.voice`) at a pitch set per NPC (`voiceOf` in `main.ts`). Interact or confirm finishes the line, then advances to the next.
  - `audio.ts` has `Ambience` (wind, crowd, water, fire crackle, birds by day, crickets at night, all levels driven by the surroundings) and `Music`.
  - `Music` plays tracks listed in `assets/audio/music/music.json`, by mood: explore, town (2+ traders within 30 m, or a crowd; markets win over night), night, sacred (near temples, shrines and tombs, or reading an inscription), danger (foes within 40 m that haven't engaged), combat, boss and stronghold.
  - **Two soundtracks** (`soundtracks` in music.json, each with its own folder): `original` (Kevin MacLeod, CC BY 4.0) and `lmms` (the user's LMMS pieces in `music/lmms/`; some instruments come from SoundFonts with an unclear licence, so it's for prototype use). `lmms` is the default. Settings → Soundtrack switches them, and the choice is saved (`Settings.soundtrack`); until the player picks one (`soundtrackPicked`), the default applies.
  - A mood a set has no piece for borrows another's (`FALLBACK` in `audio.ts`: sacred → explore, boss → combat). The next piece starts 3 s before the current one ends, so the reverb tails overlap. A track's `gain` trims its level in dB, and `maps` makes it three times as likely on those maps. A mood never repeats the piece it played last time when it has another, so fights alternate between Raid at Dusk and Drums of the Tribes.
  - Keep the credits in the README, `CREDITS.txt` and `music.json`.
- **Localisation (English + Arabic).** Every visible string goes through `src/i18n.ts`; never hard-code English in UI code.
  - Add the string to `src/locales/en.ts` and the same key to `src/locales/ar.ts`. `ar` is typed as `Dict` (the shape of `en`), so the build fails if the Arabic is missing.
  - Use `t('section.key', { name })` for UI text (`{name}` placeholders), `L().section` for data tables (quest text by quest id, small talk by role), and `tOr('section.' + id, fallback)` for keys built from level data ids (map titles, exits by `"map>to"`, landmarks, camps, bonfires, NPC titles and names).
  - Some English stays next to its data, and other languages override it by id: weapons and upgrades (`items.ts`, read with `weaponText` / `upgradeText`), enemy titles (`enemy.ts` KINDS), player looks (`characters.ts`), map badges (`minimap.ts`) and exit labels (`level.json`).
  - Arabic style: classical Arabic with a pre-Islamic flavour, easy to read, without harakat; only quoted verse carries them. Keep place and person names authentic (Hegra / الحجر, Dedan / ددان). Gendered titles go in `titlesF`.
  - Layout: `applyDocument()` sets `lang` on `<html>` and `dir="rtl"` on `#hud` and `#loading`. RTL rules live at the end of `style.css`; the bars, enemy bars, choice arrows and key glyphs (`.keys`) stay left to right. Amiri (Google Fonts) covers Arabic. Text that the HUD or menu built must re-render in `Game.onLangChange`.
- **Z-fighting.** The Blender export runs `fix_coplanar()` on every static mesh, so faces of different materials never share a plane (Lime ranks highest). `tools/blender/check_zfight.py` counts any remaining pairs.
- **Debug.** The backquote key toggles wireframes of the level.json colliders.
- **True third-person camera** (the default; Settings → Camera cycles third person → orthographic → perspective, and the Debug tab has the same toggle). `FollowCamera.thirdPerson`:
  - The camera sits behind the player's right shoulder. A ray against the level boxes pulls the boom in so it never clips a wall.
  - The character sits left of centre (shoulder offset 0.75 m, 1.05 m aiming). Riding (`FollowCamera.mounted`), the pivot rises to the rider's chest (+1.75 m over the horse's feet), the boom is about 1.3× longer plus 1.2 m, the shoulder offset grows to 1.15–1.45 m, so the horse doesn't fill the screen, even when looking back. Riding keeps the full up/down look (a pitch floor there once swallowed the stick's input).
  - The boom starts at 6.5 m and zooms from 1.6 m out to 22 m. Looking up, the camera slides in along the boom to stay above the ground (keeping the view direction) instead of being lifted. Aiming in third person fades the player to 50% (rider and horse to 35%) with a 4×4 dither on their own patched material copies (`seeThrough` and `FADE_GLSL` in main.ts): the remaining pixels are solid, so the character fades as one shape instead of showing its overlapping parts through each other. Old settings were switched to third person once (`Settings.camV`). The D-pad zooms (9 steps a second) and turns the camera (2.8 rad a second).
  - Mouse look uses pointer lock: the first click captures the mouse, and Esc frees it and opens the menu. The gamepad's right stick looks around. Zoom is the wheel or D-pad up/down.
  - Sensitivity and invert-Y live in the Debug tab. `Input.thirdPerson` switches the stick mapping.
- **Bow** (`composite_bow` in `items.ts`, `kind: 'bow'`). It is two-handed, so there is no shield.
  - Block (RMB / LT) draws and aims. Attack (LMB / RT) looses the arrow once it is fully drawn; pressing early queues the shot. Attack without aiming makes a quick shot with aim assist. Every arrow needs a fresh draw (`draw` seconds).
  - Aim direction:
    - third person: along the centre of the view (there is no crosshair; the aim arc shows where the arrow lands);
    - overhead camera with a mouse: at the cursor, with a ballistic solve;
    - overhead camera with a gamepad: the left stick turns the aim (you stand still), snapping to foes in a 22° cone.
  - **Controller aim assist** (third person, bow drawn, last device a gamepad; Settings → Aim assist, `Settings.aimAssist`, on by default):
    - drawing snaps the view onto the nearest visible foe within 14° (0.25 s);
    - a gentle pull follows a foe within 7°, and the right stick turns at 45% within 6° of one;
    - with a foe within 4° of the centre, the arrow is loosed on the ballistic arc that reaches its chest (`solveBallistic`).
  - The aim arc is shown as dots plus a landing ring. When the arc would hit a foe, the dots turn red, the ring hides and a small red arrow bobs above the foe pointing down (`Projectiles.aimMark`), with a soft tick (`Sfx.aimLock`) each time it settles on a new foe.
- **Prompts** sit low on screen (`.prompt`, top 74%) and fade in and out. The dismount prompt only shows after the ridden horse has stood still for 2 s (`horseStillFor`); dismounting works at any time.
  - The quiver holds 100 arrows (`progress.data.arrows`; older saves were topped up once, `quiver100`). Arrows stick in the ground, walls and bodies; walk over them to take them back (not out of a living foe). Resting at a bonfire refills the quiver.
  - Uses `BowDraw`, `BowAim` and `BowRelease` with `BowHeld` and `Quiver`. Older models fall back to a procedural pose and bow.
- **New foes.** Enemy spawns can have `kind` hound, vulture, slinger or clubman. The Debug tab spawns each next to the player.
  - Hounds chase, circle, crouch (the tell), then lunge with a bite.
  - Vultures circle out of sword reach, screech (the tell), dive, then skim low. Swords hit them only while they are low (`EnemySystem.hitArc`); arrows reach them anywhere.
  - Slingers keep 6–21 m away and throw stones (`Throw` clip). The stones are projectiles aimed with lead: roll through them or block them with the shield.
  - Clubmen use `ClubSwing` and the `Club` accessory, with hyper-armour like brutes.
  - All of them are `Foe`s (`combat.ts`). `EnemySystem.list` holds every foe; `humans` are the raiders that share attack tokens and ring slots.
- **Settings are remembered** (`settings.ts`, key `sands-of-arabia.settings.v1`). They are written when the menu closes and when the page hides or unloads. Settings → "Reset settings to defaults" restores them all, including English and the map's own time of day. Add any new option to `Settings`, `DEFAULT_SETTINGS`, `persistSettings()` and `resetSettings()`.
- **Camera matrices.** `FollowCamera.update` ends by refreshing both cameras' matrices. `lookAt()` updates the matrix from the position *before* it turns the camera, and HUD items projected before the render (enemy bars) need this frame's view.
- **Map edges.**
  - A sand-coloured underlay sheet sits below the lowest ground, so seams between the terrain and the far ground ring never show the sky.
  - In the overhead views (orthographic and perspective), every static map material fades to black beyond `level.bounds` (`edge` uniforms, `patchWorldMaterial` in `world.ts`) and the sky dome is hidden. Third person keeps the sky and the horizon.
- **Dismounting a moving horse** (above 3 m/s; `Player.leapOff`) leaps out of the saddle to the open side, carries 60% of the horse's speed through the air (no air drag while `leaping`) and, above 7 m/s, lands into a forward roll. The riderless horse runs on and slows over a couple of seconds (`Horse.idle`), and one left in mid-air falls or glides down.
